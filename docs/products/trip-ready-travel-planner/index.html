The trip, in one place

Dates in a messages thread, booking references in four different inboxes, a packing list started on the back of an envelope, and a running total that nobody quite trusts. This planner gathers it: where you are going, who is coming, how you are getting there, what you will do each day, what goes in the bag, and what it is costing.

There is a trip overview, a page for travellers and document expiry dates (not passport or card numbers), and a countdown from eight weeks out to departure day. Bookings for transport, stays, tours and car hire sit next to a cancellation deadline and whether they are paid. Two itinerary layouts cover a busy city day and a slower one that needs more room.

Packing lists cover a carry-on, a checked bag, kids, and beach or cold-weather add-ons. A travel budget and expense log keep planned and actual figures side by side. Colour in a places-visited world map, then use the journal and notes pages for what you want to remember. Every page is fillable on screen and prints cleanly in Letter or A4.

A spreadsheet that does the arithmetic

On Setup, name the trip, enter the dates, choose a home currency and list up to three travellers. Type exchange rates yourself, as “one unit of that currency equals this many units of your home currency”. The file does not fetch live rates and does not connect to any booking site.

The Itinerary and Bookings tabs keep the plan and the reservations. The Budget tab compares what you meant to spend with actuals from the Expense Log, after each amount is converted at the rate you entered. Cancellation deadlines in the next fourteen days, or already passed, are highlighted. A packing master list tracks what you intend to take and what is already in the bag.

The Cost Splitter reads who paid and how each expense is shared, then suggests who should pay whom. The Dashboard shows days to go, how much of the budget is used, unconfirmed bookings, unpaid booking totals and packing progress.

Rows marked “Example” show how it works; type over them or clear them.

A planning tool, not travel advice. It does not give visa, entry, vaccine, insurance or legal advice, and it does not state country-specific rules. Check official entry requirements for your nationality, and check health advice for your destination (for example, CDC Travelers’ Health or your own country’s health ministry). Keep passport numbers, card numbers and passwords out of the file and out of any copy you share.
